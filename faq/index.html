Child Sponsorship FAQ: Sponsoring a Child in Mexico

Sponsorship is a monthly commitment to a real household in Chiapas. These are the questions people ask before they make one — including the uncomfortable ones.

Sponsor a child in Mexico: the five questions we're asked most

What is sponsorachildmexico.org?

A way for people in the United States to sponsor a child at Casa Hogar Alegre, with a tax-deductible receipt. Three organizations make it work, each supplying something the others cannot.

Casa Hogar Alegre is the home. Elizabeth Castelazo — Mama Liz — opened it in Tuxtla Gutiérrez in 1993 with her two children, Ángel and Merari. All three are still there.

Baja Bound Ministries is a U.S. 501(c)(3), founded in 1993 and based near San Diego, that builds homes for families working the fields around Ensenada — most of them Indigenous families who migrated north from Chiapas and Oaxaca. It receives the donations, issues the receipts, and forwards the funds to Chiapas every month.

Valley Christian High School, in San Jose, California, started this. The sponsorship program was their idea, and their students travel to Chiapas.

The program exists because Casa Hogar Alegre had run for three decades on donations that arrived by word of mouth, and because Americans who wanted to give regularly had no way to do it that produced a tax-deductible receipt.

Does my $45 go directly to the child I sponsor?

It goes to the home, for every child in it. Casa Hogar Alegre pools sponsorships so that no child eats less because fewer people chose their photograph. You support a child by supporting the household they live in.

Am I the only person sponsoring this child?

No, and that is the point. If a sponsor's circumstances change, nothing changes for the child.

What happens when a child turns eighteen?

Legally, the home's responsibility ends. In practice, they can stay.

A young person who wants to continue — trade school, university — stays, as long as their conduct is good. So does someone who has just started their first job and is saving to move out. Ingrid is in her eighth semester of nursing school and still lives at the home.

That is why residents run from one year old to twenty-eight.

Are all of these children orphans?

Most are not. Nearly all have living relatives who cannot care for them, or must not. A Mexican family court placed them here, after abuse or neglect.

About Casa Hogar Alegre, a casa hogar in Chiapas, Mexico

What is a casa hogar?

Casa hogar means “home house.” In Mexico the term is used instead of orfanato — orphanage — on purpose. An orphanage is where children are kept. A casa hogar is meant to be where they live.

Read more about Casa Hogar Alegre →

Do the children go to school?

On campus, and without leaving it. Casa Hogar Alegre runs its own instruction from maternal through high school — core academics, English, music, vocational training and Bible study — plus medical care, psychological support and sport.

Trade school and university are off campus, and young people who go on to either keep their place at the home while they study.

How to sponsor a child in Mexico

How do I sponsor a child in Mexico?

Choose a child on this site and set up a monthly donation of $45. Baja Bound Ministries processes it, sends the funds to Chiapas each month, and emails you a tax-deductible receipt.

How much does it cost to sponsor a child in Mexico?

$45 a month at Casa Hogar Alegre. Comparable programs in Mexico run between $25 and $50. You can give any amount, monthly or once.

What does $45 a month provide?

It goes into what the home spends on every child:

  • Three meals a day, plus a snack during school hours
  • A place in the home's own school, from maternal through high school
  • Medical care and psychological support
  • Clothing, shoes and school materials
  • Music, sport, and time to be a child
  • Adults who know their name and are still there years later
Can I choose which child I sponsor?

Yes. Every child has a profile with their age, what they enjoy, and something about who they are. You can sponsor more than one.

How do I change or cancel my sponsorship?

Log in at donorbox.org with the Donor Login link, open your plan, and change the amount, pause it, or cancel it. No penalty, no notice period. PayPal recurring donations are managed inside PayPal instead.

Nothing changes for the child that day: because sponsorships are pooled, no child's meals rest on one person continuing to give.

Where your child sponsorship money goes

Where does my sponsorship money go?

To Casa Hogar Alegre in Tuxtla Gutiérrez, every month, through Baja Bound Ministries. It pays for food and daily care, the home's school, the staff who raise the children, medical care, and keeping the buildings standing.

How much of my donation reaches the children?

All of it, apart from the card processing fee. Baja Bound Ministries deducts only what Donorbox charges to process the payment and passes the rest on intact. It takes no commission and no administrative cut.

Is my donation tax-deductible in the United States?

Yes. Donations are received by Baja Bound Ministries, Inc., a 501(c)(3) registered in the United States, EIN 33-0775616. The receipt arrives by email immediately after each donation.

News, letters and gifts for your sponsored child

Will I hear from the child I sponsor?

Sponsors receive Casa Hogar Alegre's newsletter, written monthly from the home.

Can I write to the child I sponsor?

Yes. Letters can be sent to Casa Hogar Alegre's address in Tuxtla Gutiérrez. Write to contact@alegrecasahogar.org for the address.

Can I send a gift?

Yes, and the home asks for one thing: send something that builds a skill and can be shared. Books, colouring books, board games and balls all work. Toys that belong to one child and no one else tend not to.

What is the Broken Rib Coffee welcome gift?

Broken Rib Coffee, the program's coffee partner, sends new sponsors a welcome gift and gives them a discount on coffee bought from their own site.

Visiting the orphanage: sponsors, families and mission groups

Can I visit the child I sponsor?

Yes — and very few sponsorship programs can say that. Sponsors who have travelled to Chiapas have met the children they support. Families visit on their own, too. Write to contact@alegrecasahogar.org.

Can my church or school bring a group?

Yes. Casa Hogar Alegre hosts groups from churches and schools, but does not organize the trip itself: if your group is already planning to come to Chiapas, the home will help you arrange what you do while you are there.

Groups do light construction and maintenance, run activities, and spend time with the children — which is the part the children remember.

Is there a minimum age for visitors?

No. Families have come with babies. Schools bring high-school students with teachers accompanying them.

How do I get to Chiapas, and do I need a passport?

Every group flies into Tuxtla Gutiérrez (TGZ). Two usual routes:

  • From California — cross into Tijuana through the CBX bridge and take the direct flight TIJ–TGZ.
  • From elsewhere in the U.S. — connect through Mexico City (MEX) or Cancún (CUN).

You need a valid U.S. passport book. A passport card is not accepted for air travel.

Is it safe?

Groups from churches and schools have been coming to Casa Hogar Alegre for years, and families have come with small children. Write to contact@alegrecasahogar.org and the home will talk you through where groups stay, how they travel, and what to expect.
